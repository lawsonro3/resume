# 2026 October 6 - ICOE OEE 2026, The Hague - "From Global Resource to Global Market" session
Dr. Michael Lawson is the Group Manager for Water Power R&D at the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR, formerly NREL), where he provides technical and strategic leadership for a marine energy research portfolio of more than $20 million and leads a team of scientists and engineers working to advance wave, tidal, current, and ocean thermal (OTEC) energy technologies. He has worked on marine energy for more than 15 years, has a background in computational and experimental fluid dynamics, and was an original developer of WEC-Sim, SEA-Stack, and other open-source marine energy modeling and design tools.

Since 2015, Michael has also served as a technical advisor to the U.S. Department of Energy's (DOE) Hydropower and Hydrokinetic Office (formerly the Water Power Technologies Office), where he supported DOE's efforts to advance wave, tidal, ocean, and river current energy. He founded the Marine Energy Technology Symposium (METS) in 2013 and serves as Treasurer and Alternate U.S. Delegate to the International Energy Agency's Technology Collaboration Programme on Ocean Energy Systems (IEA-OES). He holds a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from The Pennsylvania State University.

# 2018 September 24 - For eagle project presentation to DOE
Michael Lawson has worked at NREL for over a decade and is a senior scientist in the Wind and Water power program. His research focuses on numerical modeling of wind turbines and marine energy systems using both high-fidelity CFD and reduced-order numerical modeling techniques. Over the last decade at NREL, Michael has used these numerical modeling methods to study the interactions between wind turbines and bats & eagles. Today, he will be speaking about an ongoing project that is combining state-of-the-art wind farm and atmospheric modeling tools, machine-learning-based behavior models, and eagle telemetry data to understand how Golden Eagles interact with utility-scale wind farms, with the goal of developing tools and methods for reducing Golden Eagle risk.
